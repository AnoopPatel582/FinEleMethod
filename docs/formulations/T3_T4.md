# T3 and T4 Element Formulations

FinEleMethod uses constant-strain simplex elements for ABAQUS `CPS3` and
`C3D4` models. Both formulations assume small-strain, linear isotropic
elasticity and require positively oriented, nondegenerate connectivity.

## T3 plane-stress element

The three-node triangle has two translational degrees of freedom per node and
the displacement order

$$
\mathbf{u}_e=
\begin{bmatrix}
u_1&v_1&u_2&v_2&u_3&v_3
\end{bmatrix}^{T}.
$$

Its linear shape functions produce constant physical derivatives and a
constant $3\times6$ strain-displacement matrix $\mathbf{B}$. Therefore, no
numerical quadrature is needed:

$$
\mathbf{K}_e=tA\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{D}_{ps}\mathbf{B},
$$

where $t$ is the section thickness, $A$ is the positive triangle area, and
$\mathbf{D}_{ps}$ is the plane-stress constitutive matrix.

Uniform pressure on an edge is converted to equal consistent nodal forces at
the two edge nodes. ABAQUS face numbering is `S1=(1,2)`, `S2=(2,3)`, and
`S3=(3,1)`.

## T4 three-dimensional element

The four-node tetrahedron has three translational degrees of freedom per node.
Its linear interpolation also produces a constant strain field. The
$12\times12$ stiffness matrix is

$$
\mathbf{K}_e=V\mathbf{B}^{T}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{B},
$$

where $V=\det(\mathbf{J})/6$ and $\mathbf{D}$ is the three-dimensional
isotropic constitutive matrix.

Uniform pressure on a triangular face is distributed equally among its three
nodes. The direction is normal to the face and inward toward the element.

## Verification

Focused tests verify:

- exact affine-strain recovery on axis-aligned and distorted elements;
- analytical constant-strain energy;
- consistent pressure-resultant magnitude and direction;
- rejection of reversed and degenerate connectivity.

The formulation kernels are implemented in `src/elements/t3.cpp` and
`src/elements/t4.cpp`. Global assembly, result recovery, and direct execution
of the instructor files are connected in the following development stage.
